## Concrete task-format polishing cases

Polish wording/response presentation while retaining source identity, original values, every subpart, choice cardinality, word banks, givens/worked examples, visuals and assessed action. Do not turn drawing/shading/matching/ordering into a different cognitive task. If item wording is frozen after clustering in the implementation, normalize at the earlier authorized polishing seam and retain the subsequent freeze.

1. **Measurement p4, printed p8, Q3:** Source asks to arrange three pictured plants from shortest to tallest and write 1 for shortest, 3 for tallest in boxes. Aegis-ready wording: “For each set of plants shown, write 1 below the shortest plant, 2 below the next tallest plant, and 3 below the tallest plant.” Keep each original picture set directly below the question. Do not replace pictures with invented centimetre heights.
2. **Numbers Beyond 20 p27, printed p46, Q2:** Pairs appear over two empty boxes, with instruction to write S for smaller and G for greater. Render each pair as `(a) 31 [ ] 33 [ ]`, or a correctly structured table, and retain S/G meanings. This is number comparison; decorative box shape need not survive, but the association of each response with a number must.
3. **Numbers Beyond 20 p9, printed p28, Q2:** Caterpillar circles contain a partly filled sequence. Flatten to an ordered sequence of given numbers and explicit blanks only if all positions are preserved. Q1 on the same page demands three actions: choose the represented number, represent it on an abacus, and write its name; do not reduce it to choosing the numeral.
4. **Time p4, printed p36, Q1:** “Write numbers 1–5 to show the time in sequence.” Preserve the five clocks and their labels. Clarify ranking from earliest to latest if supported by the source's intended sequence; never substitute printed clock times for clock-reading demand.
5. **Time p5, printed p39, Q4:** Watch/clock images paired with three checkbox options are ordinary source MCQs. Keep all original options and each timepiece image. Q3 on that page is a clock-to-time matching exercise and must stay matching.
6. **Personal Hygiene p5, printed p42, Q1:** Tick-right/cross-wrong checklist becomes a clean instruction “For each statement, mark ✓ if it is a correct hygiene habit and × if it is incorrect.” Retain all six statements. Do not add “Explain why” since the source does not demand it.
7. **Animals p9, printed p19, Q4:** Picture classification with W/D/P response boxes should explicitly define W = wild, D = domestic, P = pet and retain the six original animal pictures. The learner's evidence is the pictures, not newly supplied category hints.
8. **Seasons p6, printed p46, Q5:** A checkbox matrix associates six activities/preferences with season columns. Retain the complete source matrix/headers and row associations under the question. Do not detach the matrix as an unrelated image or generate a separate question for each new season. Q6 depends on a cyclic seasons illustration and its starting point; preserve both.
9. **Fractions Grade 3 p5, printed pp12-13, pizza Q1:** The topping/fraction table and pizza image are indispensable, including the worked corn example. A rebuilt KaTeX table or a clear original image must appear with the question. The table is not answer prose and must not disappear.
10. **Fractions Grade 3 p12, printed p27, cake task:** Preserve original ingredients (flour, sugar, eggs, baking powder, butter), amounts for eight people, target four people and all blanks. The complete table is part of the task. Never invent ingredients, change servings, or replace quantities to create variants.
11. **Fractions Grade 2 p4, printed pp42-43:** Shading halves/quarters, dividing a figure differently, and colouring fractions are visual construction tasks; representing them merely as “What is one half?” changes the task and is unacceptable.
12. **Division p10, printed p25, Q5:** Correct/incorrect division statements are checkbox tasks, with division symbols often corrupted in the text layer. Visual verification is necessary before polishing, otherwise `36 ÷ 6 = 5` can be corrupted into an addition statement.

## Source-only Post and restrained Pre

- Extract questions from the whole source, including poems, embedded activities, figures, sidebars, recaps, optional practice, and source-provided open/creative tasks. Do not generate new assessment questions from plain explanatory paragraphs merely to populate every concept.
- A SOURCE instruction asking the learner to compose something is still source-backed: Division p14 asks learners to write a division story using “packs / balls / boxes / each”; Time p10 asks for a personal activity chart; Seasons p3 asks whether the learner has seen snowfall. Preserve their open response nature. These are not authorization for the system to invent additional Post questions.
- Source worked examples should retain worked-example provenance and supplied givens; do not silently relabel them as newly unanswered problems or add variants.
- Genuine prerequisite anchors exist: Solar System p1 explicitly says “You know”: living on Earth, astronauts going into space, Sun/Moon/stars being in space. Continents p1 explicitly says Earth has water and landmasses and its landmasses are continents. Time p1-2 “Recollect” uses o'clock/half-past, hours/minutes/days/months; Fractions Grade 3 p2 recalls wholes/halves and matching parts of a whole. Use these plus credible grade-sequence evidence, with source provenance, to keep Pre bounded.
- Do not infer that everything under “Mind Jog”, an opening poem, or a chapter overview is prior knowledge. Grade 2 Fractions introduces halves/quarters in its chapter; Grade 3 Fractions introduces numerator/denominator, like fractions and fractions of collections. Their mere appearance in the current chapter does not prove the child learnt them earlier.
- Repeated recap statements can contribute evidence to existing concepts without duplicating the concept; retaining content does not require manufacturing “Summary” as a topic.
